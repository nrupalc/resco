# Brand Brief — Bright Roof Power Systems

**Purpose of this document:** A clean, self-contained brief that can be handed to a design team or a generative design tool to produce a brand identity *from scratch*. It deliberately contains **no pre-decided colours, fonts, logo directions, or taglines** — only the strategic context needed to make informed design choices.

---

## 1. What is Bright Roof Power Systems?

Bright Roof Power Systems ("**Bright Roof**" for short) is a rooftop-solar company based in **Hyderabad, India**. We design, finance, install, own, and operate grid-connected solar photovoltaic systems on the rooftops of residential apartment societies, and sell the electricity generated to the residents at a rate **below their existing grid tariff**, under a long-term Power Purchase Agreement.

We are a **partnership firm** in Year Zero — three registered partners, two non-registered contributors, a clear equity structure, a first pilot building, and a three-to-five-year plan to scale to a hundred installations.

We are the opposite of a solar panel *reseller*. We are a long-term utility-like *service provider*. Our customers sign 15–25 year contracts with us. That is the most important fact about who we are.

## 2. How we make money (so the brand has a job to do)

We install the solar system at **our cost**, on the customer's rooftop, under a long-term contract. We own the system for its entire operating life. The customer — the residents' welfare association / apartment owners' association — pays us a flat rate per kilowatt-hour that is ~20-25% below the grid tariff they are already paying. They save money from day one. We recover our capex over 4-7 years and earn a margin for the remaining 15-20 years of the system's life.

The brand must do two specific jobs to earn us money:

1. **Make a stranger on a society committee agree to a 20-year commitment with us, on first meeting.** This is the hardest sale in our category.
2. **Make that commitment feel like the *safe* choice, not the interesting one.** Customers who pick us because we "seem cool" churn; customers who pick us because we feel *reliable* stay for the full term.

## 3. Who we sell to (audience)

### Primary — Apartment Society Committees

- **Who they are.** A 3-to-7-person committee elected by the residents of a mid-rise apartment building in Hyderabad. Usually men and women in their 40s-60s. Secretary, President, Treasurer. Volunteers, not professionals. They serve 1-2 year terms.
- **What they care about.** Not being blamed for a bad 20-year decision. Lower monthly bills for residents. Simple paperwork. A local human to call when something breaks.
- **What they fear.** The installer disappearing after installation. The rooftop leaking. A new technology they don't understand. Upsetting fellow residents.
- **How they decide.** Slowly. In meetings. By consensus. They want to see a brand that feels like it will still exist, intact, in 2046.

### Secondary — Individual Homeowners (bungalows, villas, large private houses)

- Wealthier, higher-consumption households, 50-250+ unit monthly bills.
- Decide faster, less committee overhead, more design-sensitive.
- The brand needs to feel premium enough for a private home, not utilitarian.

### Tertiary (future) — Commercial / SME Rooftops

- Small factories, warehouses, office buildings in peri-urban Hyderabad.
- Require a separate PPA variant; mentioning here only so the brand doesn't *exclude* them. The primary audience is residential.

## 4. Where we stand against the competition

- **Volt, Tata Power Solar, and similar large RESCO players.** They install and hand over the system to the customer after payback. Then they leave. Our differentiator: we **retain ownership for the full life of the system** and keep the rate fixed. Customers never face a post-payback cliff.
- **Local EPC installers.** They sell the hardware. They are cheap, plentiful, and inconsistent. We don't compete with them on price per watt; we compete on *not having to think about solar for twenty years*.
- **Generic renewable-energy startups.** They look identical — blue-green palettes, earth-in-hands imagery, "revolution" language, stock photos of wind turbines. We must look *nothing* like these.

## 5. Category lies we refuse to tell

The Indian rooftop-solar category has been built on a specific set of visual and verbal clichés that we refuse to repeat. A designer should treat this list as a **hard prohibition**:

- The wind turbine. We do not operate wind turbines.
- The green leaf. We are not an environmental charity. Our proposition is financial.
- The blue globe / earth-in-hands. Ditto.
- The generic "sustainability" photograph — children running through a field at sunset.
- "Revolution" / "disruption" / "next-gen" language.
- Gradient-heavy "tech-startup" branding that implies this is an app.
- The stock-photo handshake of businesspeople in suits.
- Wordmarks set in the default fonts most startups reach for out of habit.

## 6. What we want people to feel

In one word: **inevitable**.

Like electricity from the grid itself. Like the building's plumbing. Like something that was always going to be there, never in the way, never asking for attention.

In three sub-feelings:

- **Trust, earned by longevity.** Nothing flashy. The visual language should imply that whoever is on the other end of the PPA will still be there when the person signing it is retired.
- **Warmth, rooted in the home.** The customer's roof is their roof. We are guests on it. The brand should acknowledge that.
- **Clarity, borne of specificity.** Numbers, timeframes, units, rupees. Not vague claims of impact.

## 7. What we are

- An **Indian** company, proudly local, based in Hyderabad. Not a global-agnostic "solar startup" with a Silicon Valley voice. A designer should feel permission to draw from Indian design heritage — architectural, typographic, printed matter, graphic — without kitsch.
- A company of **people you will meet.** The partner team is physically present in Hyderabad. The brand should feel human-scale, not corporate.
- A company for the **long term.** We are not optimising for a Series A logo refresh in 24 months. Design for 25 years.
- A company of **engineers, not marketers.** Our sales pitch is the spreadsheet. The brand should never feel like it's trying to sell you something; it should feel like the receipt for a decision already made.

## 8. What we are not

- A consumer app. We have a monitoring dashboard but the dashboard is not the product.
- A climate movement. Solar is our *means*; residents saving money is our *end*.
- A premium lifestyle brand. We are mid-market and proud of it.
- A hardware brand. We don't sell panels. We sell twenty years of electricity supply.

## 9. Where the brand lives (touchpoints, in order of importance)

1. **The Power Purchase Agreement** — the physical printed contract that sits in a society's records for 20 years. This is the single most important touchpoint. Design must survive in black and white on A4 paper, look authoritative, and not date.
2. **The one-page society pitch leaflet** — what a committee passes around at a meeting. Must communicate the financial proposition in 30 seconds.
3. **Site signage** — a small plate on the rooftop enclosure, visible to residents and maintenance staff. Must survive weather and still read at 10 metres.
4. **WhatsApp / social profile picture** — the mark, at 48 × 48 pixels, on a small screen.
5. **Letterhead, invoice, email signature.**
6. **The website.** Important, but not where the sale happens. Societies don't pick a solar installer from a website; they pick from a recommendation. The site's job is to close a decision already almost made.
7. **Field team clothing, vehicle decals, site-survey kits.**
8. **Pitch deck** — for the small number of large-society sales and for eventual investor conversations.

The touchpoints are heavily skewed toward **print and physical** rather than screen. The brand must be print-first.

## 10. Language and voice

- We write the way a senior engineer would explain a deal to a neighbour over tea.
- Short sentences. Specific numbers. No slogans disguised as sentences.
- Rupees, not ₹. Units, not kWh, in consumer copy (kWh is for the spec sheet).
- "We" and "you." Not "the customer," not "end-users," not "the consumer segment."
- No exclamation marks. No emoji in official copy.
- Customer-facing copy in **English** by default. Prepared for **Telugu** variant where needed (apartment signage, society-level leaflets). **Hindi** as a third tier.

## 11. Constraints the design must respect

- **Partnership firm** — not a private limited company, not a startup. Typography and document design must respect the legal form.
- **India, 2026** — the brand must feel contemporary to an Indian customer, not to a San Francisco designer.
- **Hyderabad specifically** — a city of terracotta rooftops, neem trees, cream-plastered buildings, warm golden light, flat-roofed mid-rise apartment blocks. The design system should feel *of this place*, not placeless.
- **Long documents** — the PPA is 25+ pages. The brand typography must handle long reading comfortably.
- **Small-screen viewing** — society members will view us first on WhatsApp. The mark must work at favicon size.
- **No external type licensing budget** — prefer open-source typefaces (SIL / Open Font License).
- **Must render without a designer in the room** — partners will produce invoices, contracts, and social posts themselves. The system must be usable by non-designers. Ideally codifiable as a short rules sheet.

## 12. The name itself

**"Bright Roof Power Systems"** was chosen deliberately. "Bright" carries warmth and optimism; "Roof" grounds us physically in the customer's home; "Power Systems" communicates engineering seriousness and plural product lines (we may add wind, storage, and hybrid later).

Visual identity should give the designer room to emphasise any of the three words. "Bright Roof" is the short form and is free to be treated typographically as its own wordmark.

## 13. Deliverables requested

A complete brand system, including:

1. **Logo** — primary mark, plus a badge/avatar variant for small-size and social use. Resolve how the sun (our primary symbol, given that we generate electricity from sunlight) should be represented — or argue convincingly for a different visual anchor.
2. **Colour system** — a palette with meaningful role assignments (primary, accent, supporting, neutrals, states). Must be legible on both print and screen and on both light and dark backgrounds.
3. **Typographic system** — at minimum: a display face, a body face, and a monospace / numeric face for spec sheets. Justified choices, open-source-only.
4. **Voice and tone guidelines** — with before-and-after examples.
5. **Core templates** — PPA cover, letterhead, invoice, one-page society leaflet, social avatar, WhatsApp profile image.
6. **Usage rules** — clear space, minimum size, what not to do.
7. **Supporting assets** — a small library of icons or illustrations (the things we show on a leaflet), and a photographic style guide (what our marketing photos should look like).

## 14. What success looks like

- A partner can open any of the core templates, type in the customer's name and a few numbers, and produce a document that looks like it came from a company that has existed for a decade.
- A society secretary, handed the one-page leaflet, can summarise the proposition to their wife that evening without mentioning Bright Roof's branding at all — because the branding got out of the way of the proposition.
- In five years, nothing about the system feels dated or requires a refresh.
- A competitor can never copy the look without it being obvious.

## 15. What a decision-maker needs from the designer

At delivery, we need:

- **One clear primary direction.** Not three "interesting options." A single recommended system, with the reasoning.
- **A fallback direction**, as a sanity check — one materially different alternative so we have something to compare the primary against.
- **A rationale document**, not a mood board. Explain each choice as a trade-off.

*End of brief.*
