# Three Ways An Apartment Society Can Go Solar

## Option 1: Society Buys The System

The society collects money from residents, pays for installation, owns the system, and manages maintenance.

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Paid by society/residents |
| Ownership | Society |
| Maintenance | Society must manage |
| Savings | Potentially higher after payback |
| Risk | Society carries installation, repair, replacement, and performance risk |
| Best for | Societies comfortable collecting capex and managing the system |

## Option 2: Payback-And-Handover Model

A provider installs the system and may hand it over after recovering cost or after a defined period.

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Usually low or nil depending on model |
| Ownership | May transfer later |
| Maintenance | May shift to society after handover |
| Savings | Depends on contract |
| Risk | Society may inherit system responsibility later |
| Best for | Societies that want eventual ownership and can manage long-term O&M |

## Option 3: Bright Roof Model

Bright Roof installs, owns, operates, and maintains the system. The society pays for solar electricity under the signed agreement.

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Nil for the society |
| Ownership | Bright Roof |
| Maintenance | Bright Roof handles it |
| Savings | Starting proposal of 10% against agreed benchmark |
| Risk | Bright Roof carries long-term system responsibility as defined in agreement |
| Best for | Societies that want savings without upfront collection or maintenance burden |

## Simple Decision

Choose society-owned solar if your residents want to fund and manage the system.

Choose Bright Roof if your society wants lower electricity cost without upfront installation payment and without taking over long-term system maintenance.

## First Step

Bright Roof only needs bill review and rooftop/electrical survey permission before preparing a site-specific proposal.
